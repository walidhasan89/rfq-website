Your most experienced employees should be making decisions, solving customer problems, protecting margins, and winning business.

They should not spend hours copying product descriptions from PDFs into spreadsheets.

Yet this is exactly what happens in many small and medium-sized businesses.

A salesperson receives an RFQ through [Gmail](https://mail.google.com) or Outlook. They download several attachments, identify the requested items, enter quantities into a quotation template, search for prices, calculate totals, and format a PDF.

The work must be accurate, but much of it is repetitive.

When skilled employees become manual data processors, your business pays a high salary for low-value administrative work. Quote turnaround slows, employee frustration increases, and valuable sales opportunities may be lost before the quotation is even sent.

Digitizing RFQ intake helps solve this problem.

It turns unstructured RFQ emails and attachments into a more organized workflow, allowing employees to focus on pricing, technical review, negotiation, and customer communication.

## What Is RFQ Intake?

**RFQ intake is the process of receiving, reviewing, organizing, and preparing a customer’s Request for Quotation for pricing and response.**

The process begins when an RFQ arrives and continues until the relevant information is ready for quotation preparation.

RFQ intake may involve:

* Reading the customer’s email
* Identifying the submission deadline
* Downloading attachments
* Reviewing product lists
* Extracting part numbers
* Recording quantities
* Checking specifications
* Confirming delivery requirements
* Identifying missing information
* Assigning the request to the correct employee

In a manual workflow, employees complete most of these tasks by switching between email, spreadsheets, PDFs, drawings, and internal pricing files.

Digitized RFQ intake structures the information earlier, reducing the amount of repetitive work required before pricing begins.

## Why Do Skilled Employees End Up Doing Data Entry?

Most businesses do not intentionally design a workflow where experienced salespeople or estimators become data entry clerks.

It develops gradually.

At first, the company receives only a few RFQs. One employee can process them using Gmail, Excel, and a quotation template.

As the business grows, RFQ volume increases, but the workflow stays the same.

More requests now mean more:

* Emails to review
* Attachments to download
* Product rows to copy
* Prices to check
* Documents to format
* Files to rename
* Follow-ups to remember

The company may assume this is simply part of the employee’s job.

However, the administrative portion continues growing until it consumes a significant percentage of the employee’s day.

A task that once took 20 minutes occasionally can become several hours of repetitive work every week.

## The Real Cost of Using Highly Paid Employees for Data Entry

The cost is not limited to the employee’s salary.

It also includes opportunity cost, slower sales activity, delayed quotations, employee burnout, and reduced business capacity.

## 1. You Pay Skilled-Labor Rates for Administrative Work

A salesperson, engineer, estimator, procurement specialist, or account manager may have years of industry experience.

Their value comes from their ability to:

* Understand customer requirements
* Evaluate technical feasibility
* Calculate profitable pricing
* Identify commercial risks
* Negotiate terms
* Recommend alternatives
* Build relationships
* Close deals

Copying 50 line items from an Excel attachment into a quotation template does not use those skills.

When a highly paid employee spends an hour performing repetitive data entry, the company is paying a premium rate for work that software could partially organize.

The problem becomes more expensive as RFQ volume increases.

## 2. Valuable Work Is Delayed

Every hour spent on data entry is an hour unavailable for higher-value activities.

The employee may not have time to:

* Follow up on open quotations
* Contact a promising lead
* Review a high-value opportunity
* Negotiate supplier pricing
* Speak with an existing customer
* Analyze lost deals
* Improve quotation margins
* Resolve technical questions

This is the opportunity cost of manual RFQ intake.

It does not always appear in payroll reports, but it directly affects revenue.

A salesperson who spends most of the morning preparing documents may have fewer customer conversations that day.

An estimator who repeatedly copies product details may have less time to evaluate whether the quote is commercially safe.

## 3. Quote Turnaround Becomes Slower

Customers often request quotes from several suppliers.

They may evaluate pricing, delivery, availability, terms, and responsiveness.

A manual intake process delays the quotation before pricing has even begun.

Employees must first:

* Find the correct email
* Open the attachments
* Determine which files are current
* Copy customer information
* Enter product details
* Record quantities
* Check the deadline
* Organize specifications

Only then can they begin the real commercial work.

While your team is still preparing the RFQ data, another supplier may already be sending a complete quotation.

A faster competitor does not always have better products or lower prices.

They may simply have a more efficient intake process.

## 4. Manual Data Entry Creates Avoidable Errors

Highly experienced employees are still human.

Repetitive work creates fatigue, and fatigue increases the chance of mistakes.

Common RFQ intake errors include:

* Missing a line item
* Entering the wrong quantity
* Copying an incorrect part number
* Using an outdated attachment
* Recording the wrong delivery date
* Missing a specification
* Using the wrong customer details
* Overlooking the quotation deadline

One small intake error can affect the entire quotation.

For example, an incorrect quantity may change unit pricing, shipping, packaging, and profit margin.

The employee may then need to reopen the original RFQ, correct the information, update the quotation, regenerate the PDF, and request approval again.

This rework consumes even more skilled employee time.

## 5. Employees Experience Data Entry Burnout

Highly paid employees do not automatically become immune to repetitive-work fatigue.

In fact, frustration may be greater when their skills are not being used properly.

A salesperson may feel that the company hired them to sell but expects them to spend most of the day formatting documents.

An engineer may be asked to review specifications but instead spends hours organizing files.

An estimator may have valuable pricing knowledge but loses time correcting spreadsheet rows.

Common signs of data entry burnout include:

* Reduced concentration
* Slower processing
* Increased frustration
* Avoiding large RFQs
* Rechecking the same information
* More frequent mistakes
* Lower motivation
* Working after hours to complete quotes

Burnout is not only an employee wellbeing issue.

It becomes an operational and revenue problem.

## 6. Your Business Handles Fewer Opportunities

Manual RFQ intake creates a capacity limit.

One employee can process only a certain number of requests each day.

As volume increases, the business must choose between:

* Slower turnaround
* Longer working hours
* Reduced accuracy
* Additional hiring
* Ignoring lower-value RFQs

This means the company may generate more leads than it can process.

Marketing succeeds, customers send inquiries, but the quotation workflow becomes a bottleneck.

Digitizing intake allows the existing team to handle more opportunities without increasing manual work at the same rate.

## What Does Digitizing RFQ Intake Mean?

**Digitizing RFQ intake means using software to organize and structure information from incoming RFQ emails and attachments before employees begin pricing and decision-making.**

It does not mean removing employees from the quotation process.

It means reducing the need to manually reconstruct every request.

A digitized workflow may help capture:

* Customer name
* Contact information
* RFQ reference
* Submission deadline
* Product descriptions
* Part numbers
* Quantities
* Delivery information
* Notes
* Commercial requirements

Employees can then review, edit, and approve the information.

The difference is important.

Automation prepares the data. Employees apply judgment.

## Manual RFQ Intake vs. Digitized RFQ Intake

### Manual intake

A typical manual process may look like this:

1. Open the email.
2. Read the message.
3. Download every attachment.
4. Search for the latest file.
5. Open the spreadsheet or PDF.
6. Copy product descriptions.
7. Copy part numbers.
8. Enter quantities.
9. Add customer details.
10. Create quotation rows.
11. Check for missing information.
12. Begin pricing.

This workflow requires constant switching between windows and tools.

### Digitized intake

A more structured workflow may look like this:

1. Open the RFQ email.
2. Review organized customer and product information.
3. Confirm the line items and quantities.
4. Edit any incorrect or missing details.
5. Apply pricing and commercial decisions.
6. Generate the quotation.
7. Review and send.

The employee remains responsible for accuracy.

However, they no longer need to rebuild the request manually from the beginning.

## Which RFQ Intake Tasks Should Be Digitized?

Not every task should be automated.

The best approach is to digitize repetitive, rule-based activities while keeping important decisions under human control.

### Good tasks to digitize

These may include:

* Capturing customer information
* Structuring product line items
* Recording quantities
* Reusing company information
* Applying quotation templates
* Adding brand settings
* Calculating basic totals
* Generating PDF documents
* Preparing standard response emails

### Tasks that still need human judgment

Employees should continue reviewing:

* Technical feasibility
* Product substitutions
* Unit pricing
* Profit margins
* Discounts
* Availability
* Delivery commitments
* Payment terms
* Commercial risk
* Final customer communication

Digitization should make expert work easier.

It should not attempt to replace expertise.

## Why Spreadsheets Alone Do Not Solve the Problem

Spreadsheets are useful for calculations, product lists, and pricing analysis.

However, they often become part of the manual intake problem.

An employee may still need to copy data from the email into the spreadsheet, then copy the same information into a quotation template.

Spreadsheets may also create:

* Multiple file versions
* Broken formulas
* Inconsistent formatting
* Local copies
* Overwritten data
* Limited attachment visibility
* Manual status tracking

The issue is not that spreadsheets are bad.

The issue is using them as the connection between every part of the RFQ process.

A digitized workflow can still use spreadsheet data where useful, while reducing repeated copying and disconnected file handling.

## How to Start Digitizing Your RFQ Intake Process

You do not need to replace your entire procurement system at once.

Start with the most repetitive bottlenecks.

## Step 1: Map the current workflow

Document what happens from the moment an RFQ arrives until the quotation is sent.

Identify:

* Who receives the email
* Who reviews attachments
* Who enters line items
* Where pricing is stored
* Who approves the quote
* How the PDF is created
* How follow-up is tracked

## Step 2: Measure manual time

Track how long employees spend on:

* Email review
* Attachment searching
* Data entry
* Document formatting
* Corrections
* Final review

This reveals where digitization can create the greatest return.

## Step 3: Standardize the quotation process

Create consistent rules for:

* RFQ references
* Line-item fields
* Customer information
* File names
* Pricing approvals
* Quote templates
* Terms and conditions
* Response emails

Automation works better when the underlying process is clear.

## Step 4: Digitize repeated intake fields

Focus first on information employees repeatedly copy:

* Names
* Addresses
* Product descriptions
* Part numbers
* Quantities
* Dates
* Notes

## Step 5: Keep review and approval visible

Employees should be able to check and edit the information before it becomes part of the final quotation.

## Step 6: Measure the improvement

After digitizing, compare:

* Average quote turnaround
* Employee processing time
* Correction rate
* Number of RFQs handled
* Follow-up consistency
* Quote win rate

The goal is measurable operational improvement, not automation for its own sake.

## Do SMBs Need Enterprise RFQ Software?

Not always.

Large procurement platforms may provide advanced approvals, supplier portals, analytics, and integrations.

These features can be valuable for large organizations.

However, many SMBs have a simpler problem.

They receive RFQs through Gmail or [Outlook](https://outlook.live.com/) and need to create accurate quotations faster.

A lightweight solution may be more suitable when the business:

* Has a small sales or procurement team
* Receives most RFQs by email
* Uses spreadsheets and PDF templates
* Needs faster quotation preparation
* Wants minimal setup
* Does not need a full supplier portal
* Wants employees to stay inside familiar tools

The right solution should match the actual workflow.
