Replying to an RFQ may seem simple.

A customer sends a request, your team checks the details, prepares a quotation, attaches the PDF, and replies through [Gmail](https://mail.google.com) or Outlook.

But small mistakes in this process can cost you the deal.

Customers often request quotes from several suppliers at the same time. They compare more than price. They also notice response speed, accuracy, professionalism, clarity, and how easy each supplier is to work with.

A late, confusing, or incomplete reply can remove your company from consideration before negotiations even begin.

Here are five common RFQ email mistakes that suppliers, distributors, manufacturers, wholesalers, and B2B sales teams make every day, along with practical ways to fix them.

## What Is an RFQ Email Reply?

**An RFQ email reply is a supplier’s formal response to a customer’s Request for Quotation. It normally includes pricing, quantities, product details, delivery terms, payment conditions, quote validity, and a professional quotation document.**

A strong RFQ response should make it easy for the buyer to understand:

* What you are offering
* How much it will cost
* When you can deliver
* Which terms apply
* How long the quote remains valid
* What the buyer should do next

The goal is not simply to send a price.

The goal is to give the customer enough confidence and information to move forward.

## Why RFQ Email Replies Matter So Much

Your quotation response is often one of the first real experiences a potential customer has with your business.

A well-prepared reply shows that your company is responsive, organized, accurate, and reliable.

A poor response creates the opposite impression.

Even when your price is competitive, the customer may choose another supplier if your quote arrives late, contains errors, or requires several follow-up questions.

The following five mistakes can quietly reduce your RFQ conversion rate.

## Mistake 1: Taking Too Long to Respond

Slow response time is one of the most damaging RFQ mistakes.

Customers often contact several suppliers because they need to compare options quickly. If your quote takes three days while a competitor replies within a few hours, the buyer may already be discussing the order with someone else.

### Why slow replies lose deals

A delayed quotation can make the customer think:

* Your company is too busy
* Your team is disorganized
* Order processing may also be slow
* Communication will be difficult
* You are not interested in the opportunity

This may not be true, but perception matters.

Even when the RFQ requires more time, silence creates uncertainty.

### What causes slow RFQ responses?

Manual processes are usually the main reason.

Employees may need to:

* Search through long email threads
* Download several attachments
* Copy product details into spreadsheets
* Check supplier prices
* Calculate margins
* Create a quotation document
* Review the PDF
* Write the response email

The team may also be waiting for internal approval or missing information from the customer.

### How to fix it

Create response targets based on RFQ complexity.

For example:

* Simple RFQ: respond within two hours
* Standard RFQ: respond within one business day
* Complex RFQ: acknowledge immediately and provide a clear timeline

When the final quote is not ready, send a brief acknowledgment.

Confirm that you received the request and explain when the customer can expect your quotation.

This keeps communication active and shows that the opportunity is being handled.

### Improve the workflow, not just the deadline

Telling employees to reply faster will not solve the problem if the process is still manual.

Reduce unnecessary work by using:

* Standard quotation templates
* Saved company information
* Reusable terms and conditions
* Structured pricing fields
* Automated calculations
* Professional PDF generation
* Reusable email templates

Faster response should come from a better process, not rushed employees.

## Mistake 2: Sending an Incomplete or Inaccurate Quote

An RFQ response must match the customer’s request.

A missing product, wrong quantity, incorrect model number, or outdated specification can make the entire quote unreliable.

### Common quotation errors

Employees may accidentally include:

* The wrong product code
* An incorrect unit price
* Missing line items
* Wrong quantities
* Incorrect currency
* Old delivery information
* The wrong customer name
* Incorrect taxes
* Outdated payment terms
* A quotation intended for another customer

These errors often happen because employees manually copy information from emails, PDFs, spreadsheets, and attachments.

### Why small errors create big problems

A buyer may receive several quotations that appear similar.

If one supplier sends a clean, accurate document and another sends a quote with mistakes, the choice becomes easier.

Errors also create additional work for the customer.

Instead of reviewing your offer, the buyer must contact you, explain the mistake, and wait for a corrected version.

Some customers will not bother.

They will simply move on to another supplier.

### How lost attachments cause inaccurate quotes

RFQ details may be spread across several messages.

The customer may send:

* An updated Excel file
* Revised quantities
* A new drawing
* A changed delivery address
* An additional product list
* Corrected technical specifications

If your team uses the first attachment instead of the latest one, the final quote may be wrong.

### How to fix it

Use a pre-send checklist for every RFQ.

Confirm:

* Customer details are correct
* Every requested item is included
* Product codes match
* Quantities are accurate
* The latest attachments were used
* Prices and totals were checked
* Currency is correct
* Taxes and shipping are included
* Delivery terms are clear
* Quote validity is shown

Automation can reduce copying errors, but human review is still important.

The best workflow uses automation for repetitive tasks and employees for final decisions and approval.

## Mistake 3: Writing a Weak or Confusing Email

A professional PDF quote is important, but the email around it matters too.

Many businesses send messages such as:

“Please see attached.”

That may technically complete the task, but it does not help the customer understand the offer or take the next step.

### Why a weak email reduces conversions

The buyer may have received several quotations.

Your email should make the important information easy to understand without forcing the customer to open every attachment immediately.

A confusing reply can create uncertainty about:

* What is attached
* Whether all requested items are included
* When delivery is possible
* How long the quote is valid
* Who to contact with questions
* What the customer should do next

### What a strong RFQ email should include

Your response should briefly mention:

* The customer or project name
* The RFQ reference number
* Confirmation that the quote is attached
* Important delivery information
* Quote validity
* Any exclusions or assumptions
* A clear next step
* Your contact information

Keep the message short and easy to scan.

### Example of a stronger RFQ reply

Thank you for sending RFQ #1048.

Please find our quotation attached for the requested products. The quote includes the listed quantities, estimated delivery time, payment terms, and validity period.

Please review the attached PDF and let me know if you would like us to adjust the quantities, delivery schedule, or commercial terms.

We are ready to answer any questions and help you move forward.

This message is still concise, but it feels more helpful and professional.

### Avoid these common email problems

Do not:

* Write a long, unnecessary introduction
* Use complicated language
* Hide important conditions
* Forget the RFQ reference
* Send an attachment without explanation
* Use an unclear subject line
* Leave the customer without a next step

Your email should reduce friction, not create more questions.

## Mistake 4: Sending an Unprofessional Quotation Document

Your quotation is a sales document.

It should not look like an unfinished spreadsheet or a quickly edited Word file.

A professional quote helps customers understand the offer and builds trust in your company.

### Signs of an unprofessional quotation

Common problems include:

* Poor alignment
* Inconsistent fonts
* Missing logo
* Low-quality branding
* Crowded tables
* Broken PDF formatting
* No quotation number
* Missing validity date
* Unclear totals
* Missing payment terms
* Incorrect contact details
* Unprofessional file names

A file called `quote-final-new-2.pdf` does not create a strong impression.

Use a clear file name such as:

`CompanyName-Quotation-RFQ-1048.pdf`

### What a professional quotation should include

Every quote should clearly show:

* Your company name and logo
* Contact information
* Customer name and address
* Unique quotation number
* RFQ reference
* Issue date
* Expiration date
* Product or service descriptions
* Quantities
* Unit prices
* Discounts
* Taxes
* Shipping charges
* Total amount
* Payment terms
* Delivery terms
* Notes and conditions

The document should be easy to read on both desktop and mobile devices.

### Why branding matters

A branded quotation makes your business look established and consistent.

Customers may compare documents from several suppliers. A clean, polished quote can make your company feel more reliable, even before the buyer reviews every price.

Professional design will not replace competitive pricing or good service.

However, poor design can weaken a strong offer.

### How to fix it

Create one approved quotation template for the entire team.

Use consistent:

* Brand colors
* Logo placement
* Fonts
* Table styles
* Terms
* Contact details
* File naming rules

Employees should not rebuild the document from scratch for every RFQ.

A quote-generation tool can make this process faster while keeping every document consistent.

## Mistake 5: Failing to Follow Up After Sending the Quote

Many businesses send the quotation and wait.

They assume the customer will reply when ready.

This is a costly mistake.

Customers may be busy, reviewing several offers, waiting for internal approval, or unsure about part of your proposal. A professional follow-up can bring the opportunity back to their attention.

### Why customers do not reply immediately

Silence does not always mean rejection.

The customer may:

* Need management approval
* Be comparing suppliers
* Have questions about delivery
* Want revised quantities
* Be waiting for project confirmation
* Have overlooked your email
* Need better payment terms
* Be reviewing technical requirements

Without follow-up, you may never learn what is preventing the sale.

### When should you follow up?

The ideal timing depends on the urgency and value of the RFQ.

A simple process could be:

* First follow-up: two business days after sending
* Second follow-up: five to seven days later
* Final follow-up: before the quotation expires

Do not send repetitive messages every day.

Each follow-up should be polite and useful.

### What should a follow-up email say?

Keep it simple.

Confirm that the customer received the quote, ask whether they need clarification, and offer to revise quantities, delivery, or commercial terms where possible.

For example:

I wanted to follow up on the quotation we sent for RFQ #1048.

Please let me know if you have any questions about the pricing, delivery schedule, or product specifications. We would be happy to review any changes required for your project.

This approach feels professional rather than aggressive.

### Track every sent quotation

Do not depend only on memory.

Record:

* Date sent
* Quote value
* Expiration date
* Current status
* Next follow-up date
* Customer questions
* Required revisions
* Final result

Follow-up discipline can improve sales without generating more leads.

You are simply making better use of existing opportunities.

## Other RFQ Email Mistakes to Avoid

The five mistakes above cause the most damage, but several smaller issues can also hurt your results.

### Using a vague subject line

A subject such as “Quote” is difficult to find later.

Use something specific:

`Quotation for RFQ #1048 - Company Name`

This helps both your team and the customer organize the conversation.

### Replying from the wrong thread

Starting a new email can separate the quotation from the original RFQ details.

Whenever possible, reply inside the existing conversation so the customer can see the full context.

### Forgetting quote validity

Prices, availability, and shipping costs can change.

Always include an expiration date or validity period.

### Hiding important conditions

Do not surprise the customer later with minimum order quantities, excluded shipping, payment requirements, or long lead times.

State important conditions clearly.

### Sending the wrong attachment

This can happen when employees prepare several quotes at once.

Always open and verify the attached file before sending.

### Using outdated company information

Old addresses, phone numbers, payment details, and terms can create confusion.

Maintain one approved source for company information.

## How to Build a Better RFQ Email Workflow

A reliable RFQ process should be simple enough for employees to follow consistently.

### Step 1: Identify and organize the RFQ

Assign a reference number and confirm the customer, deadline, requested items, and attachments.

### Step 2: Review all requirements

Check the entire email thread and confirm that the latest files are being used.

### Step 3: Structure the information

Organize products, quantities, specifications, dates, and customer details into clear fields.

### Step 4: Prepare pricing

Add unit prices, discounts, taxes, shipping, and margins.

### Step 5: Generate a professional quotation

Use an approved branded template and create a clean PDF.

### Step 6: Complete a pre-send review

Check the customer details, line items, totals, terms, and attachment.

### Step 7: Send a clear response

Mention the RFQ reference, summarize the offer, and provide a next step.

### Step 8: Schedule the follow-up

Record when the quote was sent and when the customer should be contacted again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should an RFQ email response include?

An RFQ response should include a clear email message and a detailed quotation showing products, quantities, prices, delivery terms, payment terms, validity, and company information.

### How quickly should a business respond to an RFQ?

Simple RFQs should ideally receive a response within a few hours. Standard requests should usually be answered within one business day. Complex requests should be acknowledged immediately with an expected delivery timeline.

### Why do businesses lose RFQ deals through email?

Common reasons include slow responses, incorrect quotes, missed attachments, unclear emails, unprofessional documents, and poor follow-up.

### How can I write a professional RFQ reply?

Thank the customer, reference the RFQ number, confirm the quotation is attached, mention important terms, and provide a clear next step.

### Should an RFQ response include a PDF quotation?

Yes. A professional PDF makes pricing, quantities, terms, and company information easier to review and share internally.

### How long should an RFQ quote remain valid?

The validity period depends on pricing stability, product availability, and industry conditions. Many businesses use a fixed period such as 15, 30, or 60 days.

### When should I follow up after sending a quote?

A first follow-up can usually be sent after two business days. Additional follow-ups may be sent later based on the project timeline and quotation expiration date.

### How can businesses avoid sending the wrong quotation attachment?

Use clear file names, keep each RFQ in a structured record, and open the attached PDF for a final review before sending.

### Can RFQ automation reduce email mistakes?

Yes. RFQ automation can reduce repetitive copying, improve document consistency, organize quote information, and speed up response preparation.
