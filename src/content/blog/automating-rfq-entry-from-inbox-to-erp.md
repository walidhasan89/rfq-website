Sales representatives should spend their time understanding customers, preparing competitive offers, and closing deals.

They should not spend hours copying information from RFQ emails into an ERP system.

Yet this is a common workflow for manufacturers, distributors, wholesalers, and B2B suppliers.

A customer sends an RFQ through [Gmail](https://mail.google.com) or Outlook. The message may contain customer details, product descriptions, quantities, delivery requirements, and a submission deadline. Additional information may be hidden inside PDF files, spreadsheets, drawings, or later replies.

The sales rep must then organize the request and manually enter it into the company’s ERP system.

This may involve creating or finding the customer record, entering product line items, confirming units, adding delivery information, attaching files, and creating a quotation or sales opportunity.

Every manual step takes time and creates another chance for error.

Automating RFQ entry from the inbox to the ERP can reduce repetitive data entry, improve quote turnaround, and give sales teams more time for customer-facing work.

However, the workflow must be designed carefully.

Automation should organize and validate RFQ information without creating duplicate customers, incorrect line items, unrealistic delivery promises, or unapproved prices.

## What Is Inbox-to-ERP RFQ Automation?

**Inbox-to-ERP [RFQ automation](https://rfqautopilot.com/rfq-automation-software/) is the process of capturing information from incoming quotation requests and transferring structured data into an enterprise resource planning system with less manual entry.**

The workflow may connect:

* Gmail or [Outlook](https://outlook.live.com/)
* RFQ attachments
* Customer records
* Product catalogs
* Pricing data
* Inventory information
* Sales quotations
* Approval workflows
* Order-management systems

A well-designed process can help turn an unstructured email into an organized ERP record.

The automation may prepare or transfer:

* Customer name
* Contact details
* RFQ reference
* Product descriptions
* Part numbers
* Quantities
* Units of measurement
* Delivery address
* Required date
* Customer notes
* Supporting attachments

The sales representative then reviews the information before pricing, approval, and customer response.

## Why Manual RFQ Entry Creates Problems

Manual ERP entry may appear manageable when the company receives only a few requests.

As RFQ volume increases, the process becomes a bottleneck.

## Repetitive Data Entry

The same information may be entered into several systems.

A sales rep may copy the customer name into:

* An RFQ tracking spreadsheet
* The ERP customer record
* A pricing worksheet
* A quotation document
* The response email

Product descriptions, quantities, and dates may also be transferred multiple times.

This repetition creates unnecessary work.

## Slow Quote Turnaround

Before the sales rep can begin pricing, they may need to:

1. Read the complete email thread.
2. Download the attachments.
3. Find the latest versions.
4. Identify product information.
5. Search for the customer in the ERP.
6. Create missing customer records.
7. Search for product codes.
8. Enter line items.
9. Add quantities and units.
10. Upload supporting files.

A competitor with a faster intake process may send its quotation while your team is still entering the request.

## Data-Entry Errors

Common mistakes include:

* Incorrect customer records
* Wrong product codes
* Missing line items
* Incorrect quantities
* Wrong units of measurement
* Duplicate RFQ records
* Outdated delivery addresses
* Missed submission deadlines
* Incorrect currencies

One error can affect pricing, inventory checks, delivery planning, and the final quotation.

## Lost Attachments

The ERP record may contain the line items but not the original technical files.

When drawings and specifications remain only inside the inbox, other employees may not have the information needed to review the request.

## Data Entry Burnout

Sales representatives may handle dozens of similar requests.

Repeatedly copying information from email into ERP forms creates fatigue and frustration.

As concentration falls, mistakes become more likely.

## Limited Selling Time

Every hour spent entering RFQ data is an hour that cannot be used for:

* Customer calls
* Follow-up
* Negotiation
* Product recommendations
* Account development
* Pipeline management
* Closing opportunities

The hidden cost of manual RFQ entry is not only administrative labor.

It is also lost selling capacity.

## What an Automated Inbox-to-ERP Workflow Looks Like

A practical workflow usually includes several controlled stages.

## Step 1: Identify the Incoming RFQ

The system or employee identifies that the email is a genuine quotation request.

Common signals include:

* RFQ in the subject
* Request for quotation
* Please provide pricing
* Product or part-number lists
* Requested quantities
* Submission deadline
* Attached specifications

Not every pricing question should automatically create an ERP quotation.

The sales rep should be able to confirm that the message is a real opportunity before transferring data.

## Step 2: Extract the RFQ Information

The email and supported attachments are reviewed for relevant details.

The extracted information may include:

* Customer company
* Contact person
* Contact email
* Phone number
* Billing or delivery address
* RFQ number
* Date received
* Submission deadline
* Product descriptions
* Part numbers
* Quantities
* Units
* Delivery requirements
* Special instructions

The information should appear in an editable review screen before it enters the ERP.

## Step 3: Match the Customer Record

The system searches the ERP for an existing customer using fields such as:

* Company name
* Email domain
* Contact email
* Customer number
* Address
* Phone number

This step is important because automatic customer creation can produce duplicates.

For example, the ERP may already contain:

* ABC Manufacturing
* ABC Manufacturing LLC
* ABC Mfg.
* ABC Manufacturing Dallas

An automated system should not assume these are four different companies.

When confidence is low, the sales rep should choose the correct record manually.

## Step 4: Match Product Codes

The incoming RFQ may use:

* Your company’s SKU
* The customer’s part number
* A manufacturer part number
* A written product description
* A previous quotation reference

The system should try to match the requested item with the correct ERP product record.

However, product matching should not depend only on similar descriptions.

“Stainless steel valve” may refer to several sizes, grades, pressure ratings, or configurations.

When no reliable match exists, the item should be marked for review rather than mapped automatically.

## Step 5: Normalize Quantities and Units

Customers may request products using different units, such as:

* Pieces
* Boxes
* Cartons
* Kilograms
* Meters
* Sets
* Pallets
* Hours

The ERP may store the product using another base unit.

For example:

* Customer requests 10 cartons.
* Each carton contains 50 units.
* ERP inventory is stored by individual unit.

The automation must understand or request confirmation of this conversion.

A unit mistake can create a quotation that is 50 times too high or too low.

## Step 6: Create a Draft RFQ or Quote Record

After validation, the system creates a draft record in the ERP.

The record may include:

* Customer
* Contact
* RFQ reference
* Requested date
* Sales representative
* Currency
* Product lines
* Quantities
* Delivery address
* Customer notes
* Attached files

The record should remain in a draft or review status.

Automation should not immediately send a quotation to the customer.

## Step 7: Add Pricing and Availability

The ERP may provide:

* Standard selling prices
* Customer-specific prices
* Cost information
* Inventory availability
* Supplier lead times
* Quantity discounts
* Tax rules

The sales rep should review these values before completing the offer.

Standard pricing may be applied automatically when the business has clear rules.

However, additional review may be required for:

* Large quantities
* Custom products
* Low inventory
* Special discounts
* New customers
* Expedited delivery
* International orders
* Long payment terms

## Step 8: Complete Technical and Commercial Review

The sales rep or relevant specialists confirm:

* Product compatibility
* Specifications
* Availability
* Production capacity
* Lead time
* Unit price
* Margin
* Shipping
* Taxes
* Payment terms
* Quote validity
* Warranty
* Customer requirements

This is where human judgment creates value.

Automation reduces administrative preparation, but employees remain responsible for the offer.

## Step 9: Approve the Quotation

Approval rules may depend on:

* Total quote value
* Discount percentage
* Gross margin
* Customer credit status
* Payment terms
* Delivery risk
* Custom products
* Contractual requirements

A routine quote may be approved by the sales rep.

A high-value or low-margin opportunity may require management or finance approval.

## Step 10: Generate and Send the Quote

Once approved, the ERP or quotation system may generate the final document.

The sales rep reviews:

* Customer details
* Line items
* Totals
* Delivery
* Terms
* Branding
* Attachments

The final response email should include the RFQ reference and a clear summary.

## Which RFQ Fields Should Be Mapped to the ERP?

Successful automation depends on clear field mapping.

A basic mapping plan may include:

| Inbox or RFQ Field       | ERP Field                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Customer company         | Customer account           |
| Contact name             | Customer contact           |
| Contact email            | Email address              |
| RFQ reference            | Customer reference         |
| Date received            | Opportunity or quote date  |
| Submission deadline      | Quote deadline             |
| Product description      | Line-item description      |
| Customer part number     | Customer item reference    |
| Manufacturer part number | Product identifier         |
| Quantity                 | Requested quantity         |
| Unit                     | Unit of measurement        |
| Delivery location        | Ship-to address            |
| Required date            | Requested delivery date    |
| Currency                 | Quote currency             |
| Notes                    | Internal or customer notes |

Not every field should be transferred automatically.

Sensitive or uncertain information may require review.

## Data Validation Rules You Should Add

Automation without validation can create errors faster than manual entry.

A strong workflow should check the data before creating the ERP record.

## Required Fields

Do not create a complete quote when essential information is missing.

Required fields may include:

* Customer
* Contact
* Product description or code
* Quantity
* Unit
* Currency
* Delivery location
* Submission deadline

The exact requirements depend on the business.

## Duplicate Checks

Before creating a new record, check for:

* Existing customer
* Existing contact
* Existing RFQ reference
* Existing draft quote
* Duplicate product lines
* Repeated email processing

This prevents the same request from being entered several times.

## Product-Match Confidence

The workflow should distinguish between:

* Exact product match
* Possible product match
* No product match

Only exact or approved matches should be entered automatically.

## Quantity Checks

Flag unusual quantities.

For example:

* Negative quantities
* Zero quantities
* Extremely large quantities
* Decimal values for products sold only as whole units

## Date Checks

Flag:

* Deadlines already passed
* Required delivery dates before the quotation date
* Unrealistically short lead times
* Missing time zones

## Currency Checks

Do not assume the currency from a symbol alone.

A dollar symbol may refer to several currencies.

The sales rep should confirm the intended currency when it is unclear.

## Customer Credit and Status Checks

The ERP may show that the customer is:

* On credit hold
* Inactive
* Overdue
* Restricted
* Missing tax information

These conditions should be visible before the quote is approved.

## How to Prevent Duplicate Customer Records

Duplicate customer records create reporting, pricing, and credit problems.

Use several identifiers when matching the company:

* Legal name
* Trading name
* Email domain
* Tax number
* Address
* Phone number
* Existing contact email

When the email comes from a new contact at an existing company, add the contact to the current customer account instead of creating a new company.

Provide employees with a simple review choice:

* Match existing customer
* Create new customer
* Mark for administrative review

Do not hide uncertain customer matching inside a fully automated workflow.

## How to Handle Products That Do Not Exist in the ERP

A customer may request a product that is not currently stored in the ERP.

Possible situations include:

* Custom product
* New manufacturer part
* Customer-specific item
* Service line
* Special-order component
* Unapproved alternative

The workflow can create a temporary description line, but it should not automatically create a permanent product master record.

Product creation may require:

* Internal SKU
* Product category
* Unit of measurement
* Cost
* Selling price
* Tax classification
* Supplier
* Inventory settings
* Approval

Product master data should remain controlled.

## Human Approval Points That Should Remain

Not every part of the inbox-to-ERP process should be automated.

## Customer Matching

An employee should confirm uncertain matches.

## Product Matching

Technical or product staff should review unclear items and substitutions.

## Pricing

Authorized employees should confirm selling prices, discounts, and margins.

## Delivery

Inventory, production, purchasing, and shipping conditions may affect delivery dates.

## Payment Terms

Customer-specific or extended terms may require finance approval.

## Technical Compliance

Engineering or product specialists may need to review drawings and specifications.

## Final Communication

A sales rep should review the quotation and customer email before sending.

## Common Inbox-to-ERP Automation Mistakes

## Automating Every Email

Not every customer question should create a quotation record.

Allow employees to confirm the request type.

## Creating Customers Automatically

Uncontrolled customer creation leads to duplicates.

## Trusting Product Descriptions Too Much

Similar descriptions may refer to different specifications.

## Assuming Requested Dates Are Approved

A customer’s required date is not the same as a confirmed delivery date.

## Automatically Applying Discounts

Discounts should follow approved rules.

## Ignoring Attachment Versions

The latest spreadsheet or drawing may appear later in the email thread.

## Sending Quotes Without Review

A professionally formatted document may still contain inaccurate data.

## Adding Too Many Steps

Automation should simplify the workflow.

If employees must correct every field or enter the same information again, the implementation has failed.

## How to Implement Inbox-to-ERP RFQ Automation

## Step 1: Map the Current Process

Document every step between receiving the email and creating the ERP quotation.

Identify:

* Repeated data entry
* Approval delays
* Duplicate records
* Product-matching problems
* Attachment issues
* Common corrections

## Step 2: Start With Standard RFQs

Begin with simple requests involving:

* Existing customers
* Existing products
* Standard prices
* Normal quantities
* Standard terms

Do not begin with complex custom manufacturing requests.

## Step 3: Define Field Mapping

Document exactly how email and RFQ fields connect to ERP fields.

## Step 4: Create Validation Rules

Define what should happen when data is:

* Missing
* Unclear
* Duplicated
* Unmatched
* Outside normal limits

## Step 5: Keep Draft Status Mandatory

Automatically created records should remain drafts until reviewed.

## Step 6: Test With Real Examples

Use different RFQ formats, including:

* Email-only requests
* PDF attachments
* Spreadsheet lists
* Revised quantities
* Existing and new customers
* Standard and unknown products

## Step 7: Train Sales Reps on Exceptions

Employees need to understand what automation can and cannot determine.

## Step 8: Measure Results

Track:

* RFQ entry time
* Quote turnaround
* Data-entry errors
* Duplicate records
* Number of RFQs processed
* On-time submission
* Quote win rate
* Employee satisfaction

## Do Small Businesses Need a Direct ERP Integration?

Not always.

A direct integration can be useful when the business:

* Receives high RFQ volume
* Uses a stable ERP process
* Has controlled customer and product data
* Needs centralized reporting
* Has technical resources for implementation
* Can maintain the integration

However, direct integration may be excessive when:

* RFQ volume is low
* The ERP is heavily customized
* Product matching is complex
* Customer data is inconsistent
* The team needs a faster solution immediately
* Most administrative time is spent preparing the quote rather than entering the ERP

A business can begin by digitizing RFQ intake and quotation preparation before building a full ERP connection.

This allows the team to standardize the process and identify which fields should eventually be integrated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is inbox-to-ERP RFQ automation?

It is a workflow that captures information from incoming RFQ emails and transfers validated customer, product, quantity, and delivery data into an ERP system.

### Can an email automatically create an ERP quotation?

Yes, when an integration exists. However, the record should usually remain a draft until a sales rep verifies the data, pricing, availability, and terms.

### How does automation prevent duplicate customers?

It can compare company names, email domains, contact emails, addresses, and customer identifiers before creating a new record.

### Can AI match customer part numbers to ERP products?

It can assist, especially when mapping data already exists. Uncertain or technically sensitive matches should be reviewed manually.

### Should prices be entered automatically?

Standard ERP pricing may be applied automatically, but employees should review discounts, margins, customer-specific prices, and unusual quantities.

### Can requested delivery dates be transferred automatically?

They can be recorded as customer-requested dates. They should not become confirmed delivery promises without availability or production review.

### What happens when a product is not in the ERP?

The system may create a temporary quotation description, but permanent product-master creation should follow controlled approval rules.

### Does inbox-to-ERP automation replace sales representatives?

No. It reduces data entry so sales reps can focus on customer requirements, pricing, follow-up, negotiation, and closing.

### Do small businesses need a full ERP integration?

Not always. Small teams may begin with structured RFQ intake and [quotation automation](https://rfqautopilot.com/quotation-email-automation/), then add direct integration when volume and process maturity justify it.

### Does RFQ AutoPilot connect directly to every ERP?

RFQ AutoPilot is primarily focused on organizing RFQs and preparing quotations inside Gmail and Outlook. ERP entry or integration depends on the company’s systems and implementation.
