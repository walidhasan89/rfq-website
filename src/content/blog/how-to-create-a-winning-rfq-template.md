A strong Request for Quotation template can save your business hours of repetitive work.

Instead of rewriting the same email, searching for missing details, or correcting unclear requests, your team can use one consistent structure every time it asks suppliers for pricing.

A well-designed RFQ template also helps vendors respond faster and more accurately.

When suppliers receive clear product descriptions, quantities, deadlines, delivery requirements, and pricing instructions, they are less likely to ask unnecessary questions or submit incomplete quotes.

This guide explains how to create a professional RFQ template for products, services, and vendor sourcing. You will also find free RFQ email templates that you can copy, customize, and send.

## What Is an RFQ Template?

**An RFQ template is a reusable document or email format that businesses use to request prices, delivery details, payment terms, and other commercial information from suppliers.**

It gives every vendor the same instructions and requirements.

This makes supplier quotations easier to compare because each business responds to the same request.

A standard RFQ template can include:

* Buyer and supplier information
* RFQ reference number
* Product or service descriptions
* Required quantities
* Technical specifications
* Delivery location
* Requested delivery date
* Pricing instructions
* Payment terms
* Quote submission deadline
* Required attachments
* Contact information

The template should be detailed enough to prevent confusion but simple enough for suppliers to review quickly.

## Why Small Businesses Need a Standard RFQ Template

Many small businesses manage procurement through [Gmail](https://mail.google.com), Outlook, spreadsheets, and PDF documents.

An employee may write a new RFQ email every time the company needs products or services.

This creates several problems.

Important information may be forgotten. Different employees may use different formats. Suppliers may receive unclear instructions, and quotations may become difficult to compare.

A standard template creates a more reliable process.

### It reduces repetitive work

Employees do not need to write every request from scratch.

They can reuse the same structure and replace only the customer, product, quantity, date, and project information.

### It improves supplier responses

Clear RFQs help suppliers understand exactly what they need to quote.

This reduces follow-up questions and speeds up the response.

### It makes quotations easier to compare

When every vendor receives the same requirements, you can compare:

* Unit price
* Total price
* Delivery time
* Payment terms
* Warranty
* Shipping charges
* Product availability
* Quote validity

Without a consistent RFQ, suppliers may send offers based on different assumptions.

### It reduces purchasing errors

A structured template reminds employees to include important details such as quantities, specifications, deadlines, and delivery addresses.

This reduces the chance of ordering the wrong product or accepting an inaccurate quotation.

### It creates a professional impression

A clear and organized RFQ shows suppliers that your business has a serious purchasing process.

Professional requests are more likely to receive timely and complete responses.

## What Makes an RFQ Template Successful?

A winning RFQ template does more than ask, “How much does this cost?”

It gives suppliers everything they need to prepare a useful commercial offer.

The best RFQ templates are:

* Clear
* Specific
* Complete
* Easy to scan
* Consistent
* Deadline-driven

## Essential Elements of a Professional RFQ Template

## 1. A Clear RFQ Subject Line

The email subject should immediately explain what the message contains.

Avoid vague subjects such as:

* Price needed
* Quote request
* Urgent
* Product inquiry

Use a subject like:

**RFQ #2048: Request for Pricing for Industrial Fasteners**

Or:

**Request for Quotation: 500 Custom Packaging Boxes**

A clear subject helps suppliers prioritize the request and find it later.

## 2. A Unique RFQ Number

Assign a reference number to every request.

For example:

* RFQ-2026-0015
* RFQ-WH-2048
* RFQ-MKT-105

Use the same number in the:

* Email subject
* RFQ document
* File names
* Internal records
* Vendor responses
* Final purchase order

A unique reference prevents confusion when several quotations are active at the same time.

## 3. Buyer Information

Include the name and contact details of the company requesting the quote.

Add:

* Company name
* Contact person
* Email address
* Phone number
* Billing address
* Delivery address
* Website, when relevant

Suppliers should know who is making the request and where the order may need to be delivered.

## 4. Supplier Information

Leave a section for the supplier to complete.

This may include:

* Supplier name
* Sales representative
* Email
* Phone number
* Business address
* Tax or registration number
* Quotation reference

This information helps your team identify and compare responses.

## 5. Product or Service Description

Describe each requested product or service clearly.

Do not use descriptions such as:

* Standard model
* Regular size
* Good quality
* Same as before

These descriptions can mean different things to different suppliers.

Include details such as:

* Product name
* SKU or part number
* Brand
* Model
* Size
* Material
* Color
* Technical specification
* Required certification
* Scope of work
* Expected outcome

The more specific the request, the more accurate the quotation.

## 6. Required Quantity

List the exact quantity for every item.

When the final quantity is uncertain, request pricing for several quantity levels.

For example:

* 100 units
* 500 units
* 1,000 units

This helps your business understand volume discounts and make a better purchasing decision.

## 7. Unit of Measurement

Always identify the unit.

Examples include:

* Each
* Box
* Carton
* Kilogram
* Meter
* Hour
* Day
* Project
* Monthly service

Without a clear unit, a supplier may quote per item while another quotes per package.

## 8. Pricing Fields

Tell suppliers exactly how pricing should be presented.

Ask for:

* Unit price
* Total price
* Currency
* Discount
* Tax
* Shipping cost
* Installation cost
* Setup fees
* Recurring charges
* Additional expenses

This prevents vendors from hiding major costs outside the main price.

## 9. Delivery Requirements

Include complete delivery information.

Specify:

* Delivery address
* Required delivery date
* Acceptable delivery window
* Shipping method
* Incoterms, when applicable
* Partial delivery rules
* Packaging requirements
* Urgency level

The cheapest supplier may not be the best option if they cannot meet your deadline.

## 10. Payment Terms

Ask vendors to state their payment requirements.

Common payment terms include:

* Payment in advance
* Net 15
* Net 30
* Net 60
* Deposit plus final payment
* Milestone payments
* Cash on delivery
* Letter of credit

Payment terms can significantly affect cash flow and the real value of an offer.

## 11. Quote Validity

Prices can change due to material costs, currency movements, shipping rates, or product availability.

Ask the supplier to state how long the quotation remains valid.

Examples include:

* 15 days
* 30 days
* 60 days
* Until a specific date

A validity period helps your team avoid approving an expired price.

## 12. Warranty and Support

When relevant, request information about:

* Product warranty
* Replacement policy
* Maintenance
* Technical support
* Service-level agreements
* Spare parts
* Returns
* Training

The lowest price may not provide the best long-term value.

## 13. Submission Deadline

Every RFQ should have a clear deadline.

Write the exact date and time, including the time zone when vendors are in different locations.

For example:

**Please submit your quotation by July 30, 2026, at 5:00 PM Eastern Time.**

Avoid phrases such as “as soon as possible.”

A clear deadline creates urgency and makes your process easier to manage.

## 14. Submission Instructions

Tell suppliers exactly how to respond.

Include:

* Email address
* Required file format
* Email subject
* Documents to attach
* Pricing format
* Contact person for questions

For example:

**Please reply to this email with your quotation attached as a PDF. Use “Quotation for RFQ-2026-015” as the subject line.**

## 15. Evaluation Criteria

You do not need to reveal your complete selection process, but it can help to mention the main factors.

For example:

* Price
* Delivery time
* Product quality
* Warranty
* Supplier experience
* Payment terms
* Availability
* Technical compliance

This helps suppliers submit more relevant offers.

## How to Write an RFQ Email

A good RFQ email should be short.

The detailed requirements can appear in the email body, an attached document, or a spreadsheet.

The email should:

1. Introduce the request.
2. Mention the RFQ reference.
3. Summarize the required products or services.
4. State the deadline.
5. Explain how to submit the quotation.
6. Invite relevant questions.

Do not include a long company history or unnecessary marketing language.

The supplier needs to understand the request quickly.

## Common RFQ Template Mistakes

Even a professional-looking RFQ can fail if important information is missing.

### Using unclear product descriptions

A supplier should not need to guess which product, model, size, or quality level you need.

Add part numbers, specifications, drawings, or reference images when possible.

### Leaving quantities open

Suppliers cannot provide accurate pricing without quantities.

When quantities are not final, ask for several pricing levels.

### Forgetting the deadline

Without a submission deadline, quotations may arrive too late for your decision.

### Ignoring shipping costs

A low product price can become expensive after freight, handling, customs, and packaging charges are added.

Ask suppliers to separate these costs.

### Requesting price only

Price is important, but so are delivery time, warranty, availability, payment terms, and quality.

### Sending too many attachments without explanation

Clearly name every attachment and explain what it contains.

For example:

* Attachment 1: Product list
* Attachment 2: Technical specifications
* Attachment 3: Delivery requirements

### Using outdated templates

Review your RFQ template regularly.

Update contact details, addresses, terms, branding, and submission instructions.

## How to Compare Vendor Quotations

Once responses arrive, create a structured comparison.

Compare each supplier based on:

* Unit price
* Total landed cost
* Shipping
* Taxes
* Delivery time
* Payment terms
* Quote validity
* Product compliance
* Warranty
* Supplier experience
* Customer support
* Risk

Do not automatically choose the lowest price.

A slightly more expensive supplier may provide better delivery, stronger warranty protection, or more reliable quality.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should an RFQ template include?

An RFQ template should include buyer details, an RFQ number, product or service descriptions, quantities, specifications, pricing fields, delivery requirements, payment terms, quote validity, and a submission deadline.

### What is the purpose of an RFQ template?

An RFQ template standardizes how a business requests pricing from suppliers. It helps vendors provide complete quotations and makes received offers easier to compare.

### Can I send an RFQ by email?

Yes. Many small businesses send RFQs through Gmail or Outlook. The requirements can appear in the email body or in attached PDF and spreadsheet files.

### How long should suppliers have to respond to an RFQ?

The deadline depends on the complexity of the request. Simple RFQs may allow several business days, while complex technical requests may require one or more weeks.

### Should an RFQ include a budget?

It is optional. Including a budget can help suppliers recommend suitable options, but some buyers prefer not to reveal it during the initial pricing stage.

### What is the difference between an RFQ and a quote?

An RFQ is the buyer’s request for pricing. A quote is the supplier’s response containing prices, terms, delivery details, and validity.

### Should shipping and taxes be included in an RFQ response?

Yes. Suppliers should clearly identify shipping, taxes, duties, handling fees, and any other costs so the buyer can calculate the total purchase cost.

### How should RFQ files be named?

Use the company name, RFQ reference, document type, and date. For example: `CompanyName-RFQ-2048-Product-List-2026-07-20.xlsx`.

### Can an RFQ template be used for services?

Yes. A service RFQ should include the scope, deliverables, schedule, estimated workload, pricing format, support requirements, and completion deadline.
