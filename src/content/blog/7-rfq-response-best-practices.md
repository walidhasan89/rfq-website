RFQs are not ordinary sales leads.

A customer who sends a Request for Quotation has already identified a need. They may have a budget, specifications, quantities, and a deadline. In many cases, they are actively comparing suppliers and preparing to make a purchase.

That makes every RFQ a high-intent sales opportunity.

Yet many sales teams treat RFQ responses as administrative work.

They copy product information into spreadsheets, build a quotation from an old template, attach the PDF, and send an email that says:

“Please see attached.”

This approach may deliver a price, but it does not create a strong buying experience.

A winning RFQ response should help the customer understand your offer, trust your company, compare your value, and take the next step quickly.

The following seven best practices can help sales representatives respond faster, improve quote quality, and convert more RFQs into revenue.

## What Makes an RFQ Response Successful?

**A successful RFQ response is accurate, fast, easy to evaluate, commercially clear, and focused on the buyer’s decision.**

It should answer:

* Did the supplier understand the requirement?
* Is the offered product or service compliant?
* What is the complete cost?
* When can the supplier deliver?
* Why should the buyer choose this offer?
* What happens next?

The lowest price does not always win.

Customers also consider:

* Reliability
* Availability
* Delivery speed
* Communication
* Warranty
* Technical support
* Payment terms
* Supplier credibility
* Ease of doing business

Your response should make these advantages visible.

## Best Practice 1: Respond Faster Than Your Competitors

Speed is one of the strongest advantages in RFQ sales.

A fast response shows that your company is organized, attentive, and interested in the opportunity.

It also gives your sales representative more time to communicate with the buyer before the final decision.

### Why speed affects RFQ conversion

Buyers often send the same request to several suppliers.

The first complete response may become the reference point for:

* Expected pricing
* Delivery
* Product alternatives
* Commercial terms
* Technical questions

A supplier that responds early can help shape the conversation.

A supplier that responds late may find that the buyer has already shortlisted another vendor.

### Fast does not mean careless

An inaccurate quotation sent in 10 minutes is not better than an accurate quotation sent later.

The goal is to reduce unnecessary delays such as:

* Searching through email threads
* Copying customer details
* Rebuilding quotation tables
* Finding approved templates
* Formatting PDFs
* Writing the same response email repeatedly

Employees should spend their time reviewing pricing, availability, specifications, and risk.

### Set internal response targets

Create service standards based on RFQ complexity.

For example:

* Simple product RFQ: Same business day
* Standard multi-line RFQ: Within one business day
* Technical or custom RFQ: Acknowledge quickly and provide a clear timeline
* Urgent stock request: Respond within a few hours

Even when the full quotation needs more time, acknowledge the request.

### RFQ acknowledgment example

**Subject: RFQ #[Number] Received**

Hi [Buyer Name],

Thank you for sending RFQ #[Number].

We are reviewing the requested products, quantities, and delivery requirements. We expect to submit our complete quotation by [date and time].

I will contact you promptly if we need any clarification.

Best regards,

[Name]

This reassures the buyer that the request is being handled.

## Best Practice 2: Qualify the Opportunity Before Investing Hours

Not every RFQ has the same value or probability of success.

Some requests are an excellent fit. Others may have unrealistic deadlines, unsuitable specifications, or little commercial potential.

Sales teams should qualify RFQs before investing significant time.

### Questions to ask

Review:

* Can we supply the requested product or service?
* Can we meet the deadline?
* Is the order value worthwhile?
* Is the customer a good fit?
* Do we have competitive pricing?
* Are the payment terms acceptable?
* Is the RFQ complete?
* Are we an approved or credible supplier?
* Is the buyer seriously evaluating vendors?
* Does the opportunity support future business?

### Use a simple priority system

RFQs can be grouped into categories such as:

* **High priority:** Strong fit, high value, realistic deadline
* **Standard priority:** Good fit, moderate value, normal complexity
* **Review required:** Missing information, low margin, unusual risk
* **No bid:** Poor fit, impossible requirement, unacceptable terms

This helps the sales team protect time.

### Decline professionally when necessary

Ignoring an RFQ can damage the relationship.

When you cannot quote, send a short response.

**Subject: RFQ #[Number] – No Bid**

Dear [Buyer Name],

Thank you for inviting us to quote for RFQ #[Number].

After reviewing the requirement, we are unable to provide a suitable offer because [brief reason].

We appreciate the opportunity and would be happy to review future requests that align with our capabilities.

Best regards,

[Name]

A professional no-bid response keeps the door open.

## Best Practice 3: Follow the Buyer’s RFQ Structure

Buyers want quotations that are easy to compare.

If the RFQ contains 30 numbered line items, your response should follow the same order whenever possible.

Do not force the buyer to work out which product matches each requested line.

### Mirror the buyer’s structure

Include:

* Original item numbers
* Customer part numbers
* Product descriptions
* Requested quantities
* Units of measurement
* Compliance status
* Your offered price
* Delivery information

A useful quotation table may look like this:

| RFQ Item | Description             | Quantity | Offered Product     | Unit Price | Lead Time |
| -------- | ----------------------- | -------: | ------------------- | ---------: | --------- |
| 1        | Stainless steel bracket |      500 | Exact match         |   USD 8.50 | 4 weeks   |
| 2        | Mounting hardware kit   |      500 | Exact match         |   USD 1.20 | In stock  |
| 3        | Protective cover        |      250 | Alternative offered |   USD 4.75 | 2 weeks   |

### Clearly identify alternatives

Do not quietly replace the requested item.

State:

* What was requested
* What you are offering
* Why it is different
* Whether approval is required

For example:

**Item 3 is quoted using Model PC-220 because the requested PC-210 is discontinued. The proposed alternative has the same dimensions but requires technical approval.**

This protects trust.

### Explain unquoted lines

If an item is unavailable, mark it clearly.

Use:

* No bid
* Discontinued
* More information required
* Unable to meet delivery
* Alternative available on request

Missing lines make the quote look incomplete.

## Best Practice 4: Sell Value, Not Just Price

Many RFQ responses provide numbers but do not explain why the buyer should choose the supplier.

This is a missed sales opportunity.

The buyer may receive several quotations with similar prices. Your response should communicate the commercial value behind your offer.

### Valuable differences may include:

* Faster delivery
* Local inventory
* Better warranty
* Lower shipping cost
* Technical support
* Flexible order quantities
* Reliable past performance
* Better payment terms
* Strong quality controls
* Easier returns
* Custom packaging
* Dedicated account support

### Use specific value statements

Weak:

“We provide excellent service and high quality.”

Strong:

“We currently have 300 units available for immediate shipment, allowing you to begin the project while the remaining quantity is produced.”

Weak:

“We are a reliable supplier.”

Strong:

“Our offer includes a 12-month warranty, full product traceability, and a dedicated account contact for order updates.”

Specific statements are more credible.

### Connect value to the buyer’s goal

Do not list features without explaining the benefit.

Instead of:

“We offer local stock.”

Write:

“Local stock reduces transit time and helps protect your required installation date.”

Instead of:

“We provide technical support.”

Write:

“Our technical team can review the proposed component before order approval, reducing the risk of compatibility issues.”

### Add a short value summary

A quotation may include a section called:

**Why This Offer Works**

* 300 units available immediately
* Remaining quantity delivered within four weeks
* Shipping included
* Net 30 payment terms
* 12-month warranty
* Dedicated technical support

Keep it short and factual.

## Best Practice 5: Make Pricing Completely Transparent

Confusing pricing slows purchasing decisions.

The buyer should not need to calculate your final cost or ask whether freight, tax, tooling, or setup is included.

### Every quotation should show:

* Quantity
* Unit price
* Extended price
* Currency
* Subtotal
* Discounts
* Shipping
* Taxes
* Additional charges
* Grand total

### Separate additional costs

List costs such as:

* Tooling
* Setup
* Packaging
* Shipping
* Installation
* Testing
* Training
* Maintenance
* Recurring fees
* Customs duties

For example:

| Cost            |         Amount |
| --------------- | -------------: |
| Product total   |      USD 9,500 |
| Tooling         |      USD 1,200 |
| Shipping        |        USD 450 |
| **Grand total** | **USD 11,150** |

### Explain the pricing basis

State whether the offer depends on:

* Order quantity
* Annual volume
* One complete shipment
* Standard packaging
* Current material costs
* Specific contract length
* Minimum order quantity

For example:

**Pricing is based on an initial order of 1,000 units and delivery in one shipment.**

### Offer quantity options

A buyer may be able to increase volume in exchange for a better price.

| Quantity | Unit Price |
| -------: | ---------: |
|      100 |  USD 12.50 |
|      500 |   USD 9.75 |
|    1,000 |   USD 8.60 |

This gives the buyer more flexibility and can increase order value.

### Protect your profit margin

Sales quotas should not encourage employees to win unprofitable deals.

Before sending, confirm:

* Product cost
* Labor
* Freight
* Overhead
* Discounts
* Payment costs
* Warranty risk
* Profit margin

Winning revenue without acceptable margin can hurt the business.

## Best Practice 6: Create a Professional, Low-Friction Buying Experience

The buyer’s experience begins before the order.

A disorganized quotation can make the buyer worry about future communication, delivery, and support.

Your response should feel easy to review and easy to approve.

### Use a professional quotation structure

Include:

* Company logo
* Company contact details
* Customer information
* Quotation number
* RFQ reference
* Issue date
* Expiration date
* Clear line-item table
* Pricing summary
* Delivery
* Payment terms
* Assumptions
* Exclusions
* Acceptance instructions

### Use a clear file name

Avoid:

* `Final Quote.pdf`
* `New Quote 2.pdf`
* `Updated Pricing Final.pdf`

Use:

`YourCompany-Quotation-RFQ-1048.pdf`

### Write a useful response email

Do not send only:

“Please see attached.”

Use a short summary.

**Subject: Quotation for RFQ #1048**

Dear [Buyer Name],

Thank you for the opportunity to respond to RFQ #1048.

Please find our quotation attached for the requested products.

Key details:

* Total: USD [Amount]
* Estimated delivery: [Date or lead time]
* Payment terms: [Terms]
* Quote validity: [Period]

Our offer is based on [specification, drawing, or quantity reference].

Please let me know if you would like us to review alternative quantities, delivery options, or product substitutes.

Best regards,

[Name]

### Reduce buyer effort

Make acceptance simple.

Tell the buyer whether they should:

* Send a purchase order
* Sign the quotation
* Approve a drawing
* Confirm the selected quantity
* Choose a delivery option
* Schedule a technical review

The easier it is to move forward, the less likely the opportunity is to become inactive.

## Best Practice 7: Follow Up With Purpose

Many RFQs are not lost because of price.

They are lost because the supplier sends the quote and never follows up.

The buyer may be:

* Waiting for internal approval
* Comparing suppliers
* Reviewing technical details
* Changing quantities
* Delaying the project
* Needing more information

A well-timed follow-up can restart the decision.

### Do not send generic follow-ups repeatedly

Avoid:

“Any update?”

Instead, offer help.

Ask:

* Do you need revised quantities?
* Would an alternative product help?
* Should we review faster delivery?
* Do you need technical documents?
* Is the project timeline still active?
* Would another payment option help?

### First follow-up example

**Subject: Follow-Up on RFQ #1048**

Hi [Buyer Name],

I wanted to confirm that you received our quotation for RFQ #1048.

Please let me know if you need any clarification, revised quantities, alternative products, or updated delivery options.

We would be happy to adjust the offer based on your current requirements.

Best regards,

[Name]

### Follow up before expiration

**Subject: Quotation Expiration – RFQ #1048**

Hi [Buyer Name],

Our quotation for RFQ #1048 is valid until [date].

Please let me know if the opportunity is still active or if you need revised pricing, quantities, or delivery information before the offer expires.

Best regards,

[Name]

### Record the next action

Every quotation should have:

* Date sent
* Current status
* Last contact date
* Next follow-up date
* Customer feedback
* Responsible sales rep

Do not depend only on memory.

## RFQ Metrics Every Sales Team Should Track

### RFQs Received

How many quotation opportunities enter the business?

### RFQs Responded To

How many receive a complete quotation?

### On-Time Response Rate

What percentage are sent before the customer’s deadline?

### Average Quote Turnaround

How long does it take to respond?

### Quote Win Rate

What percentage become orders?

### Average Quote Value

What is the typical value of each quotation?

### Average Won Order Value

Are won deals larger or smaller than the typical quote?

### Follow-Up Completion Rate

Are sales representatives completing planned follow-ups?

### Lost Reasons

Why are deals being lost?

Common reasons may include:

* Price
* Delivery
* Availability
* Technical mismatch
* Payment terms
* Slow response
* No follow-up
* Project cancelled
* Existing supplier relationship

These metrics reveal whether the team has a lead problem, pricing problem, workflow problem, or follow-up problem.

## Common RFQ Response Mistakes That Hurt Sales Quotas

## Responding Too Late

The buyer may complete the evaluation before your quote arrives.

## Quoting the Wrong Revision

A quote based on outdated specifications may be rejected.

## Sending Incomplete Pricing

Hidden or unclear fees make the offer harder to compare.

## Focusing Only on Price

The buyer may value delivery, service, warranty, or reliability more.

## Using Generic Product Descriptions

The buyer cannot confirm whether your offer meets the requirement.

## Making Unrealistic Delivery Promises

Winning a deal with an impossible commitment creates future problems.

## Sending the Wrong Attachment

Always open and check the final PDF.

## Forgetting Follow-Up

A quote without a next action can become wasted sales effort.

* Correct PDF attached
* Professional filename
* Value summary included

* Follow-up date scheduled

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes an RFQ response successful?

A successful RFQ response is accurate, complete, fast, easy to compare, commercially clear, and focused on the buyer’s next decision.

### How quickly should sales teams respond to an RFQ?

Simple RFQs should often be answered the same day. More complex requests should be acknowledged quickly with a clear submission timeline.

### Does the lowest quotation always win?

No. Buyers also consider delivery, quality, reliability, warranty, payment terms, service, and risk.

### How can sales reps make an RFQ response persuasive?

Show that you understand the requirement, highlight specific value, provide transparent pricing, offer relevant options, and make the next step easy.

### Should RFQ responses include alternatives?

Yes, when an alternative improves availability, cost, or delivery. Clearly label it and explain the difference.

### Why is follow-up important?

Buyers often need time or clarification. A useful follow-up can restart the opportunity and improve conversion.

### How many times should a sales rep follow up?

The timing depends on the deal, but a first follow-up after two business days and another before quote expiration can be effective.

Important metrics include response rate, turnaround time, win rate, average quote value, follow-up completion, and lost reasons.
