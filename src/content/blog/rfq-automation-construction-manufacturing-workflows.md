Construction and manufacturing teams depend on fast, accurate quotations.

A contractor may need pricing for electrical materials, structural steel, windows, flooring, equipment, or subcontracted work before submitting a project bid.

A manufacturer may receive an RFQ containing part numbers, drawings, quantities, tolerances, materials, and delivery requirements.

In both industries, the quotation process often begins inside [Gmail](https://mail.google.com) or Outlook.

An employee opens the email, downloads several attachments, reviews spreadsheets and PDFs, copies line items into another file, contacts suppliers, calculates prices, prepares a quotation, converts it into PDF, and writes a response.

This process may work for a few simple requests.

It becomes difficult when the business handles dozens of RFQs, large bills of materials, revised drawings, changing quantities, and urgent submission deadlines.

[RFQ automation](https://rfqautopilot.com/rfq-automation-software/) transforms the workflow by reducing repetitive administration between the incoming request and the final quotation.

It helps employees organize RFQ information, create structured line items, reuse company details, generate professional documents, and prepare responses faster.

The result is not only faster quoting.

It is a better daily workflow for estimators, sales representatives, project managers, procurement teams, engineers, and operations employees.

## The Traditional Daily RFQ Workflow

A manual workflow often looks like this:

1. The RFQ arrives through email.
2. An employee reads the message.
3. Attachments are downloaded.
4. The latest files are identified.
5. Product or project information is copied into a spreadsheet.
6. Prices are requested from suppliers.
7. Labor, materials, shipping, and margin are calculated.
8. A quotation document is created.
9. The document is converted into PDF.
10. A response email is written.
11. The final quote is sent.
12. Follow-up depends on reminders or memory.

Each step may involve a different tool.

Employees move between:

* Gmail or [Outlook](https://outlook.live.com/)
* Excel
* PDF readers
* Estimating software
* ERP systems
* Shared folders
* Word templates
* Calculators
* Supplier portals

The process becomes slow because employees act as the manual connection between all these tools.

## Managing Revised Drawings and Specifications

Revisions are common in both industries.

Construction projects may issue new addenda or plan revisions.

Manufacturing customers may change drawings, tolerances, quantities, or materials.

The risk is that an employee uses the original attachment instead of the updated version.

### Better Revision Practices

Use:

* Clear revision numbers
* Descriptive file names
* One current source of information
* Replacement instructions
* Updated deadlines
* Formal review before final pricing

For example:

**Drawing Revision C replaces Revision B. Please disregard the earlier file and submit revised pricing by the updated deadline.**

RFQ automation can improve organization, but employees must still confirm the active revision.

## What Should Not Be Fully Automated?

Construction and manufacturing quotations contain real technical and commercial risk.

Several decisions should remain under human control.

## Technical Compliance

Employees must confirm that the quoted product, material, or service meets the specification.

## Product Substitutions

An alternative may appear similar but fail to meet technical, legal, or performance requirements.

## Takeoffs and Quantities

Construction quantities and manufacturing volumes should be verified before final pricing.

## Pricing and Margin

Authorized employees should approve costs, markups, discounts, and profitability.

## Capacity

The business should confirm whether labor, machines, materials, and suppliers can support the requested schedule.

## Delivery Commitments

A customer’s requested date is not automatically an approved delivery promise.

## Contract Terms

Unusual warranties, penalties, retention, payment terms, or liability clauses may need management or legal review.

## Final Approval

The business remains responsible for the quotation, regardless of how it was prepared.

Track:

## Common RFQ Automation Mistakes

### Automating an Unclear Process

Software cannot fix weak pricing rules, inconsistent ownership, or missing approval requirements.

### Trusting Extracted Data Without Review

A clean draft may still contain incorrect quantities or product information.

### Ignoring Revisions

The latest drawing or project addendum must be confirmed.

### Automating Delivery Promises

Requested dates should be reviewed against real capacity and availability.

### Removing Technical Employees From the Workflow

Automation should reduce administration, not eliminate engineering or estimating review.

### Buying Software That Is Too Complex

A small team may not need an enterprise procurement platform.

The tool should solve the actual daily bottleneck.

### Adding Another Disconnected System

Employees should not need to enter the same information into both the old and new process.
