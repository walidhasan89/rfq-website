A professional Request for Quotation can save your business time, reduce purchasing mistakes, and help you receive more accurate prices from suppliers.

A weak RFQ creates the opposite result.

When product details are vague, quantities are missing, or delivery requirements are unclear, vendors must make assumptions. You may receive quotations based on different materials, shipping methods, timelines, or service scopes.

Those quotes become difficult to compare.

The procurement process then turns into a long chain of clarification emails, revised documents, missing attachments, and delayed decisions.

This guide explains how small businesses can write a clear, complete, and professional RFQ that suppliers can respond to accurately.

## What Is an RFQ?

**An RFQ, or Request for Quotation, is a formal document or email used to ask suppliers for pricing and commercial terms for clearly defined products or services.**

Businesses usually send an RFQ when they already know what they need.

The supplier is expected to provide information such as:

* Unit pricing
* Total pricing
* Product availability
* Delivery time
* Shipping costs
* Payment terms
* Quote validity
* Warranty information
* Additional fees

The main purpose of an RFQ is to collect comparable quotations from multiple vendors.

## When Should a Small Business Use an RFQ?

Use an RFQ when your requirements are specific enough for suppliers to calculate an accurate price.

An RFQ is suitable when you know:

* The product or service required
* The quantity
* The technical specifications
* The expected delivery date
* The delivery location
* The required quality level
* The scope of work

For example, an RFQ is appropriate when your business needs:

* 500 units of a specific product
* Custom-manufactured parts based on drawings
* Monthly cleaning services for defined locations
* Packaging with exact dimensions and materials
* Equipment installation with a clear scope
* A fixed number of software licenses
* Freight services for a known shipment

When the project is not clearly defined and you need suppliers to recommend a solution, an RFP may be more appropriate.

## Why a Professional RFQ Matters

A professional RFQ helps both the buyer and the supplier.

### It improves quotation accuracy

Suppliers can calculate pricing properly when they understand the product, quantity, quality, delivery, and commercial requirements.

### It reduces clarification emails

Complete information prevents vendors from repeatedly asking for missing details.

### It speeds up procurement

Suppliers can prepare quotes faster, and your team can compare the responses without reorganizing every offer.

### It creates fair comparisons

Every supplier receives the same requirements.

This helps prevent one company from quoting a premium product while another quotes a lower-cost alternative.

### It reduces hidden costs

Clear RFQs help expose shipping, tooling, setup, taxes, support, and other charges before you make a decision.

### It presents your business professionally

An organized request shows suppliers that your company has a serious and reliable procurement process.

## How to Write a Professional RFQ

A strong RFQ should be clear, complete, and easy to scan.

Use the following structure.

## 1. Create a Clear RFQ Title

The title should explain exactly what you are requesting.

Good examples include:

* RFQ for 500 Custom Corrugated Shipping Boxes
* Request for Quotation for Office Cleaning Services
* RFQ for CNC-Machined Aluminum Components
* Request for Pricing for 25 Business Laptops
* RFQ for Monthly Warehousing and Fulfillment Services

Avoid unclear titles such as:

* Need price
* Product quote
* Urgent request
* New project
* Supplier inquiry

A specific title helps vendors understand the opportunity immediately.

## 2. Assign a Unique RFQ Number

Every RFQ should have a unique reference.

Examples include:

* RFQ-2026-001
* RFQ-PROD-1048
* RFQ-MKT-026
* RFQ-WH-2026-15

Use the same reference in:

* The email subject
* The RFQ document
* Attachment names
* Internal spreadsheets
* Supplier responses
* Purchase orders
* Follow-up messages

A unique number keeps communication organized when multiple requests are active.

## 3. Add Your Company Information

Introduce the buyer clearly.

Include:

* Company name
* Company address
* Website
* Contact person
* Job title
* Email address
* Phone number
* Billing address
* Delivery address, when known

You may also add a short company introduction when it gives useful context.

Keep it brief.

For example:

“ABC Manufacturing is a US-based supplier of commercial equipment. We are currently sourcing packaging materials for a new product line.”

The RFQ should focus primarily on the request, not your company history.

## 4. Explain the Purpose of the Request

Write a short overview explaining what your business needs and why.

For example:

“We are requesting quotations from qualified suppliers for the production and delivery of 2,000 custom-branded retail boxes. The selected supplier may also be considered for recurring quarterly orders.”

This gives the supplier useful commercial context.

You can mention:

* Whether the purchase is one-time or recurring
* Whether it is for a new project
* Whether you are replacing an existing supplier
* Whether future volume is expected
* Whether samples or prototypes are required

Do not reveal confidential details that are not necessary for pricing.

## 5. Describe the Products or Services Clearly

This is one of the most important parts of the RFQ.

The supplier must know exactly what to quote.

For products, include:

* Product name
* SKU or part number
* Brand or approved equivalent
* Model
* Material
* Size
* Color
* Dimensions
* Weight
* Technical specifications
* Required certification
* Packaging requirements
* Quality standard

For services, include:

* Scope of work
* Locations
* Required tasks
* Expected deliverables
* Estimated workload
* Service frequency
* Completion deadline
* Required experience
* Support requirements
* Performance expectations

Avoid vague descriptions such as:

* Good-quality materials
* Standard service
* Regular size
* Similar to our current product
* Premium solution
* Fast delivery

Use measurable requirements whenever possible.

## 6. Include Accurate Quantities

Suppliers cannot calculate reliable prices without quantities.

State the exact number required for each item.

When your final volume is uncertain, ask for quantity-based pricing.

For example:

| Quantity |         Unit Price |        Total Price |
| -------: | -----------------: | -----------------: |
|      100 | Supplier completes | Supplier completes |
|      500 | Supplier completes | Supplier completes |
|    1,000 | Supplier completes | Supplier completes |
|    5,000 | Supplier completes | Supplier completes |

Quantity breaks help you understand volume discounts.

For recurring purchases, include:

* Initial order quantity
* Monthly estimate
* Annual forecast
* Expected order frequency

Be honest about forecasted volume.

Do not promise large future orders only to receive lower pricing.

## 7. Add Technical Specifications and Attachments

Some requests need more information than an email can contain.

Attach relevant files such as:

* Product specifications
* Technical drawings
* CAD files
* Images
* Material requirements
* Service scope documents
* Site plans
* Packaging artwork
* Compliance documents
* Product lists
* Previous samples

Use clear file names.

For example:

* `RFQ-1048-Product-Specifications.pdf`
* `RFQ-1048-Quantity-List.xlsx`
* `Part-AB204-Rev-C-Drawing.pdf`
* `Packaging-Artwork-Version-2.ai`

Avoid file names such as:

* Final.pdf
* New file.xlsx
* Latest version 2.pdf
* Product list updated final.xlsx

List every attachment inside the RFQ so suppliers can confirm that they received everything.

## 8. State Quality and Compliance Requirements

Quality requirements may affect material selection, production methods, inspection, and price.

Include relevant standards such as:

* ISO certifications
* Industry regulations
* Safety requirements
* Material certifications
* Environmental requirements
* Inspection standards
* Testing requirements
* Traceability
* Warranty expectations
* Country-of-origin rules

Only request requirements that are genuinely necessary.

Unnecessary certification or inspection demands can increase costs and reduce the number of eligible suppliers.

For manufactured products, you may also request:

* First article samples
* Pre-production samples
* Inspection reports
* Certificates of conformity
* Material certificates
* Test results
* Quality control plans

## 9. Define Delivery Requirements

Tell suppliers exactly when and where the order is required.

Include:

* Delivery address
* Required delivery date
* Delivery schedule
* Shipping method
* Packaging requirements
* Partial shipment rules
* Receiving hours
* Freight responsibilities
* Incoterms, when relevant

Use exact dates.

Instead of writing:

“Delivery needed urgently.”

Write:

“Complete delivery is required by September 15, 2026.”

Ask the supplier to state both:

* Production lead time
* Estimated delivery date

Lead time may begin only after final artwork, sample approval, deposit payment, or purchase order confirmation.

## 10. Request a Complete Pricing Breakdown

Do not ask only for one total price.

Request enough detail to understand the complete cost.

Ask suppliers to provide:

* Unit price
* Total product or service price
* Setup fee
* Tooling cost
* Sample cost
* Installation cost
* Packaging cost
* Shipping cost
* Taxes
* Duties
* Recurring fees
* Optional charges
* Discounts
* Minimum order quantity

A detailed breakdown makes supplier comparisons more accurate.

It also reduces the risk of unexpected charges after you select a vendor.

## 11. Specify the Required Currency

State which currency suppliers should use.

For example:

“Please submit all pricing in US dollars.”

If you accept multiple currencies, ask suppliers to identify:

* Currency used
* Exchange-rate assumptions
* Whether the price is fixed
* Whether currency changes may affect the final amount

This is especially important when purchasing internationally.

## 12. Ask for Payment Terms

Payment terms can affect cash flow and supplier risk.

Ask vendors to state:

* Deposit requirement
* Balance payment date
* Net 15, Net 30, or Net 60 terms
* Payment milestones
* Accepted payment methods
* Credit requirements
* Tooling payment terms
* Late-payment conditions

A lower quotation may not provide the best value if the supplier requires full payment in advance.

## 13. Request Delivery and Lead-Time Information

Ask the supplier to provide:

* Current availability
* Production lead time
* Sample lead time
* Tooling lead time
* Shipping time
* Estimated delivery date
* Expedited options
* Capacity limitations

Lead time should be specific.

For example:

“Production lead time: 20 business days after sample approval.”

This is clearer than:

“Delivery in approximately one month.”

## 14. Include a Quote Validity Requirement

Supplier prices may change because of:

* Raw material costs
* Freight rates
* Currency movements
* Product availability
* Labor costs
* Energy prices

Ask suppliers to state how long their quotation will remain valid.

Common periods include:

* 15 days
* 30 days
* 60 days
* 90 days

You may write:

“Please ensure that your quotation remains valid for at least 30 days from the submission date.”

## 15. Set a Clear Submission Deadline

Every RFQ should have an exact deadline.

Include:

* Date
* Time
* Time zone

For example:

“Please submit your quotation by August 10, 2026, at 5:00 PM Eastern Time.”

Avoid unclear instructions such as:

* Send soon
* Reply urgently
* Provide pricing when possible
* Respond at your earliest convenience

A clear deadline helps suppliers prioritize the request and supports fair evaluation.

## 16. Explain How Suppliers Should Respond

Give vendors clear submission instructions.

Specify:

* Email address
* Email subject
* File format
* Required quotation structure
* Supporting documents
* Contact person for questions
* Whether alternatives are allowed

For example:

“Please reply to this email with your quotation attached as a PDF. Use ‘Quotation for RFQ-2026-1048’ as the subject line.”

You may also ask suppliers to complete a pricing spreadsheet so every quote follows the same structure.

## 17. State Your Evaluation Criteria

Suppliers should understand how their quotations will be reviewed.

Common evaluation criteria include:

* Total cost
* Product compliance
* Delivery time
* Quality
* Warranty
* Payment terms
* Supplier experience
* Production capacity
* Customer support
* Previous performance
* Sustainability
* Risk

You do not need to reveal your complete scoring formula.

However, identifying the main factors helps suppliers prepare more relevant offers.

## 18. Include Terms, Assumptions, and Conditions

Your RFQ may include important conditions such as:

* The buyer is not required to select the lowest bidder.
* The buyer may reject any or all quotations.
* Suppliers are responsible for their response costs.
* Pricing must include all listed requirements.
* Alternative proposals must be clearly identified.
* Confidential information must not be shared.
* A purchase order is required before work begins.
* Submission does not guarantee a contract.

Keep these terms clear and reasonable.

For high-value or regulated purchases, ask a qualified legal professional to review them.

## Professional RFQ Structure

A complete RFQ can follow this order:

1. RFQ title
2. RFQ number
3. Issue date
4. Buyer information
5. Supplier information
6. Request overview
7. Product or service requirements
8. Quantities
9. Technical specifications
10. Quality requirements
11. Delivery requirements
12. Pricing table
13. Payment terms
14. Quote validity
15. Submission deadline
16. Submission instructions
17. Evaluation criteria
18. Terms and conditions
19. Contact information
20. Attachment list

This structure makes the document easy to review.

**Subject:** RFQ #2026-1048: Request for Quotation for Custom Packaging

[Company Name] is requesting a quotation for the production and delivery of custom packaging for our new product line.

Please review the attached RFQ and product specifications.

Key details are listed below:

**RFQ number:** RFQ-2026-1048
**Required quantity:** 2,000 units
**Delivery location:** [Address]
**Required delivery date:** September 15, 2026
**Quotation deadline:** August 10, 2026, at 5:00 PM Eastern Time

Please include:

* Unit and total pricing
* Setup or tooling costs

* Production lead time

* Payment terms
* Quote validity

Contact me before the deadline if you need clarification about the requirements.

Thank you,

[Your Name]
[Job Title]
[Company Name]
[Phone Number]
[Email Address]

## Common RFQ Writing Mistakes

## Providing incomplete specifications

Suppliers cannot quote accurately when important product or service details are missing.

Use technical documents, examples, measurements, and clear descriptions.

## Forgetting quantities

A quotation without a defined quantity may be unusable.

Provide exact or estimated volumes and request quantity breaks when needed.

## Asking for price only

The cheapest option may have longer delivery, poor payment terms, limited warranty, or high shipping costs.

Ask for the complete commercial offer.

## Using vague deadlines

“As soon as possible” does not create a shared expectation.

Use a specific date and time.

## Sending unorganized attachments

Clearly name files and explain what each one contains.

Confirm that outdated versions have been removed.

## Ignoring supplier questions

Respond to clarification questions quickly.

When one question affects all bidders, consider sharing the answer with every participating supplier.

## Changing requirements without version control

If the RFQ changes, update the revision number and clearly explain what was modified.

Do not quietly replace an attachment inside a long email thread.

## Making the RFQ unnecessarily complicated

A professional RFQ does not need to be difficult to read.

Use short sections, tables, bullets, and plain language.

## How to Compare RFQ Responses

Create one comparison sheet for all received quotations.

Compare:

* Unit price
* Total cost
* Shipping
* Taxes and duties
* Setup or tooling
* Product compliance
* Delivery time
* Payment terms
* Warranty
* Quote validity
* Supplier experience
* Risk
* Customer support

Do not compare only the first number shown on the quotation.

Calculate the total landed or project cost.

Also review whether each vendor followed your instructions and clearly stated assumptions.

## Final RFQ Checklist

Before sending the RFQ, confirm:

* The title clearly explains the request.
* A unique RFQ number is included.
* Company and contact details are correct.
* Product or service descriptions are complete.
* Quantities are stated.
* Technical files are attached.
* The correct file revisions are included.
* Quality requirements are clear.
* Delivery address and deadline are included.
* Pricing breakdown requirements are stated.
* Currency and payment terms are covered.
* Quote validity is requested.
* The submission deadline includes a time zone.
* Response instructions are clear.
* Evaluation criteria are mentioned.
* Every attachment has a descriptive file name.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What information should be included in an RFQ?

An RFQ should include buyer information, product or service descriptions, quantities, specifications, quality requirements, delivery details, pricing fields, payment terms, quote validity, and a submission deadline.

### What is the main purpose of an RFQ?

The purpose of an RFQ is to collect comparable pricing and commercial terms from qualified suppliers for clearly defined products or services.

### How long should an RFQ be?

An RFQ should be long enough to explain the complete requirement but no longer than necessary. Simple requests may fit into an email, while technical purchases may require a detailed document and attachments.

### Can an RFQ be sent by email?

Yes. Small businesses commonly send RFQs through Gmail or Outlook. Clear subject lines, structured requirements, and organized attachments are essential.

### How many suppliers should receive an RFQ?

The number depends on the purchase and industry. Many businesses request quotes from at least three qualified suppliers to compare pricing, delivery, and terms.

### Should an RFQ include a budget?

Including a budget is optional. It may help suppliers determine whether they can meet your expectations, but some buyers prefer to collect independent pricing first.

### What is the difference between an RFQ and an RFP?

An RFQ requests pricing for clearly defined requirements. An RFP asks suppliers to recommend a broader solution, approach, or strategy.

### Should an RFQ include shipping costs?

Yes. Ask suppliers to state shipping, insurance, duties, taxes, and handling separately so you can compare the total cost.

### How can I make supplier quotations easier to compare?

Send every supplier the same requirements and ask them to follow a standardized pricing format.
